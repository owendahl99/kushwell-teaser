Why Cannabis Suffers From a Knowledge Gap

Cannabis entered legalization without the research scaffolding that usually surrounds therapeutic products. Patients were left to self-experiment. Providers were left to advise without evidence. The result is confusion — and a market that defaults to shortcuts like potency and discounts.

This knowledge gap isn’t just “missing data.” It’s the absence of a disciplined learning system. Modern cannabis is complex: formulations, strains, delivery methods, and terpene profiles can change outcomes dramatically — even when THC is similar. We’re only beginning to appreciate the depth of this plant, and the industry still lacks a standard, patient-centered way to learn responsibly at scale.

  • Research lag: decades of prohibition and weak incentives left cannabis under-studied.
  • Retail-first reality: the market scaled faster than evidence and standards could follow.
  • Complexity ignored: terpenes and profiles matter, but the ecosystem rarely measures outcomes.
  • Patients bear the cost: trial-and-error becomes the default when guidance is missing.
